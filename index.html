ZONE BASED IRRIGATION SYSTEM

AI & IoT-Enabled Zone-Precision Irrigation and Automated Leak Detection System for Sustainable Agriculture

Project Proposal & Technical Documentation
Smarter Irrigation Healthier Crops A Sustainable Future
1. Executive Summary

Zone Based Irrigation System is a zone-aware, AI-assisted precision irrigation system designed to solve two critical problems: a conventional "soil moisture low → pump ON" system ignores: spatial variability of soil conditions and undetected water loss from pipe or drip-line leakage.

The system deploys solar-powered sensor nodes across independently controllable irrigation zones. Each node reports soil moisture, temperature, humidity and (optionally) soil electrical conductivity over a LoRa network to a central gateway. A cloud-based decision engine combines this data with a live weather forecast to compute zone-wise irrigation requirements and actuates solenoid valves. Only the zones that genuinely need water are watered — while dry zones stay off. In parallel, expected vs actual flow comparison detects leakage at the pump and each zone, raising instant alerts. Results and alerts are delivered via a web/mobile dashboard and SMS. The system reduces water and energy use, giving farmers real-time visibility into their land.

2. Introduction & Background

Agriculture is the largest consumer of freshwater. In Bangladesh, irrigation still relies on manual judgement, fixed schedules, or single-sensor "threshold" systems. Soil, drainage, shading and micro-terrain create zones with different moisture levels. Watering the whole field based on one sensor leads to over-watering and under-watering, wasting water, energy and damaging crops. Pipes and drip lines are rarely monitored, so leaks can go unnoticed for days, causing major losses.

Aquazone AI addresses both problems together: zone-aware irrigation and infrastructure-aware leak detection.
3. Problem Statement
  • Uniform irrigation wastes water (over- and under-watering).
  • Manual inspection does not scale (slow, subjective, impractical).
  • Leakage goes undetected (water, energy and crop loss).
  • Lack of real-time field data and zone-wise control.
4. Related Work / Existing Approaches
  • Single-sensor threshold irrigation systems
  • Manual field inspection
  • Basic flow meters without AI/analytics
  • Limited or no leak detection
  • No integration of weather forecast and zone-wise control.
5. Objectives
  • Enable zone-precise irrigation using AI and IoT.
  • Detect and alert pipe/drip-line leaks in real time.
  • Reduce water and energy consumption.
  • Provide real-time monitoring via web/app and SMS.
  • Be cost-effective and field-deployable for smallholders.

10. Limitations & Risk Mitigation
Limitations
  • ◆ Initial setup cost.
  • ◆ Dependence on weather data accuracy.
  • ◆ Network coverage challenges in remote areas.
  • ◆ Maintenance of sensors and valves.
Risk Mitigation
  • ◆ Use low-cost, scalable components.
  • ◆ Hybrid (LoRa + GSM) communication.
  • ◆ Durable, weatherproof hardware.
  • ◆ Local training and support.
11. Future Scope
  • AI-based crop health monitoring.
  • Autonomous valve control.
  • Integration with satellite imagery.
  • Expansion to larger farms.
  • Multi-crop and multi-region adaptation.
12. Conclusion

Zone Based Irrigation System addresses the real challenges of modern agriculture by combining AI, IoT and sustainable energy. It enables zone-precision irrigation, detects leaks, conserves resources and empowers farmers — building a smarter, greener and more resilient future for agriculture in Bangladesh.
